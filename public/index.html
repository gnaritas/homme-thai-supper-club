The house

Supper, the way it felt at home

Gabby Chu grew up on a farm in southern Thailand, learning to cook beside her mother and in her uncle's kitchen before coming to the United States in 2015. Homme is her invitation back to those tables — “an invitation to sit at my family's table,” as she puts it.

You feel love in here. It's just like a living room or dining room of someone's house, and we like to be the host. — Chef Vivid “Gabby” Chu, Phoenix New Times

The dining room keeps that feeling: soft butter-yellow paint, wood wainscoting, exposed brick, rattan pendants and vintage photos over tables and leather booths — and a courtyard patio strung with twinkling lights for evening meals outside.

Family recipes, deep technique

Lesser-known southern Thai dishes sit beside the classics diners love — fried chicken wings learned in an uncle's kitchen, a slow-simmered curry scented with acacia leaves, and yes, pad Thai.

A bar with its own story

Thai-inspired cocktails, Arizona-made beer and wine, and a natural wine selection — see what's on the bar list.

From the LeDu Thai kitchen

Homme is Chef Chu's second Phoenix restaurant. Her first, LeDu Thai off Roosevelt Row, was named among Phoenix New Times' best new restaurants of 2024.

Good to know

Before you come

Do you take reservations?

Yes — book a table on OpenTable. The live booking screen shows every time still open that night.

How does supper work at Homme?

Family-style. Order a few dishes, put them in the middle, and let everyone have a little bit of everything — something spicy, something comforting, something fresh, something crispy.

Is there vegetarian and gluten-free food?

Yes — the menu carries clearly marked vegetarian and gluten-free dishes, from fried soft tofu to marked salads, curries, and wok plates.

Can we sit outside?

Yes — the courtyard patio, strung with twinkling lights, is open for evening meals.

What should I wear?

Business casual: neat and put-together, comfortable rather than formal.
