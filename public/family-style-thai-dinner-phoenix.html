How supper works here

Supper in Phoenix, the family table way

Growing up in Thailand, supper was never just supper. It was gathering around the table with good people, ordering a few dishes for everyone to share, passing plates around, stealing bites from each other's plates — and always saying, “Try this one.”

At Homme that's not a gimmick; it's how the kitchen was built. Order a few dishes, put them in the middle, and let everyone have a little bit of everything — something spicy, something comforting, something fresh, something crispy. Dishes arrive designed for the middle of the table: a platter of southern Thai fried chicken wings to pick at, a slow curry to spoon over jasmine rice, a dry stir-fry to chase with cold beer.

Bring people

Good for groups, good for staying a while

Diners book Homme for birthdays, gatherings, and just-because dinners — diners' guides tag it good for groups, and the room is made for long table talk: a cozy brick-and-butter-yellow dining room with leather booths, and a twinkling courtyard patio when the Phoenix weather cooperates.

A table that flows

New plates keep the middle of the table full — salads and grilled plates to start, curries and wok dishes for the middle of the meal, noodles and sweets to slow things down.

Everyone gets enough

Marked gluten-free and vegetarian dishes ride along on every part of the menu, so a mixed group eats from the same spread.
